How it works

Three simple steps. No home Wi-Fi is needed.

📱

1. The phone feels the motion

A free app called Phyphox reads how the phone is tilted and turned, and shares these numbers over Wi-Fi.

➜
🧠

2. A small board does the thinking

An ESP32 board makes its own Wi-Fi, reads the phone's numbers, and smooths out small shakes.

➜
🎥

3. Two motors move the stand

One motor turns the stand left and right. The other tilts it up and down. It follows your phone.

You can also open a control web page on the board and move the stand with an on-screen joystick. On a computer, Python programs show a 3D phone that moves just like the real one.

    Latest changes

      Known problems

      Things we found that still need fixing. None of them stop the project from working.

      • Saved file has a wrong column. In smMotionV2.py, the "yaw" column in the saved file shows the "roll" numbers by mistake.
      • A math sign is wrong in an older program. In smartphone_motionV2.py, one small math step uses the wrong sign.
      • The older program gets stuck at some angles. The same older program acts strangely when the phone points straight up or down. The newer smMotionV2.py does not have this problem.
      • The older program gets slower over time. Its graphs keep growing and use more and more memory.
      • Errors are hidden. If the phone is not connected, the programs do not say why.
      • Settings are inside the code. You must edit the code to change the phone's address.

      What do the words mean?

      A short guide to the words used in this project.

      Roll
      Tipping the phone to the left or right.
      Pitch
      Tilting the phone forward or backward.
      Yaw
      Turning the phone left or right, like a door.
      Pan and tilt
      Pan means turning the stand left and right. Tilt means moving it up and down.
      Servo
      A small motor that can turn to an exact angle.
      ESP32
      A small, cheap computer board with Wi-Fi built in.
      Phyphox
      A free phone app that reads the phone's sensors and shares the data.
      Filter (smoothing)
      Math that removes small shakes so the motion looks calm. "Kalman" and "low-pass" are two kinds.
      FFT
      Math that finds how many times per second something shakes.
      Commit
      One saved change to the project's code.
